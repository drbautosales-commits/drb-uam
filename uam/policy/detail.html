Certification Comparison: FAA vs EASA

Summary

Both the FAA (Federal Aviation Administration) and EASA (European Union Aviation Safety Agency) have developed certification pathways for eVTOL aircraft. While both agencies aim to ensure safety, their approaches differ in categorization, timeline, and specific requirements. This document compares the key aspects of each certification framework.

Comparison Table

Aspect FAA (United States) EASA (European Union)
Primary Regulation Part 21, Special Conditions SC-VTOL (Special Condition)
Categories Powered-lift category Basic, Enhanced, Certified
Pilot Requirements Commercial Pilot License VTOL-specific license
Noise Standards Under development Defined in SC-VTOL
First Certifications Expected 2024-2025 Expected 2024-2025

Notes

  • Both agencies are working on mutual recognition agreements to facilitate international operations.
  • The certification timeline depends heavily on manufacturer readiness and testing completion.
  • Special conditions may be updated as technology matures and operational experience grows.
  • Urban operations may require additional local permits beyond federal certification.
